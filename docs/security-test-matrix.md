# CLPR security test matrix

The catalogue of attack vectors CLPR has considered, what defends against each, and where that
defence is asserted. This is the canonical list: `test/security/` implements it, and a test's name
carries its row id (`test_X1_nonOwner_reverts` asserts row X1).

Row ids are **append-only** — they are referenced from test names, so a retired row is
removed and its id is never reused.

Classification: `E` executable/new · `E*` executable/already asserted elsewhere · `D`
documented-only · `N` no defence exists · `deferred` the vector exists only under the
`ChannelSyncData` model (#400), so the row keeps its id until that lands.

Citations name a **file and a symbol**, never a line — a symbol survives edits and is greppable.
Slot numbers and byte lengths are quoted together with the constant that proves them.

61 vectors: 13 verifier, 14 bundle, 11 messaging, 11 channel, 12 access control.

## 1. Verifier attacks

The errors in `ClprTypes` are **service-layer** errors; verifiers almost never use them and declare
their own instead. The only `ClprTypes` error any verifier raises is `VerifyFailed(string)`
(HieroVerifier only). `MockAvalancheVerifier` performs **no** cryptographic verification — its
`verifyBundle` is `pure` and returns the `QueueMetadata` and payloads decoded from the proof bytes
as-is (`ClprProtobuf.decodeBundleContent`) — and
is excluded from the compliance gate by the `Mock` prefix rule in `VerifierComplianceCoverage`.

## 2. Bundle attacks

Entry chain: `ClprService.submitBundle` → `BundleLogic.submitBundle` (`nonReentrant onlyService
whenEnabled`) → `BundleLib.processBundle` → `_validateAndPrepare` / `_applyBundle`.

`BundleLib` reads local state from `_channels[channelId]`, and the verifier returns
`(QueueMetadata, messagePayloads, newTrustAnchor, newTrustAnchorId, newEndpointManifest)`. A bundle's
last message is the peer's `nextMessageId - 1`, and `sentRunningHash` is its proven running hash.

| # | Vector | Why it matters | Defence | Class | Suite / notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| B1 | No-progress bundle DoS: resubmit a bundle that advances nothing | Cheap griefing; wasted gas, event spam, log noise for relayers | `BundleLib._checkBundleProgress` reverts the BundleLib-local `NoProgress()` unless one of five criteria advances: `nextMessageId` exceeds our `receivedMessageId + 1`; a new trust anchor; a higher manifest version; the peer's `receivedMessageId` exceeds our `ackedMessageId`; or `_stepStatus` yields a status transition under a look-ahead `effectiveAcked` | `E*` | Asserted by `BundleLib_Replay.test_replayDefense_sameBundleTwice` and `BundleLib_Progress.test_bundleProgress_noProgressAtAll_reverts` |
| B2 | Rate-limit bypass: flood `submitBundle` | Unbounded submission rate | **None.** No window, counter, or timestamp state exists in `ClprServiceStorage`, and `ClprTypes.Throttles` has no rate field. Only `NoProgress` and gas cost limit a flood | `N` | File issue |
| B3 | Running-hash forgery: deliver payloads that do not match the peer's proven chain | Breaks the cross-ledger integrity chain; enables message injection. Only the peer's queue-head `sentRunningHash` is proven, so this check is what protects every payload in the bundle | `BundleLib._validateAndPrepare` Step 5 seeds `computedHash` from `channel.receivedRunningHash` (forced to zero when nothing has been received), chains `sha256(prev ‖ sha256(payload))` over the **new suffix only** — leading duplicates are already folded into the seed — and compares against the verifier-proven `QueueMetadata.sentRunningHash` → `ClprRunningHashMismatch`. Not skipped for a payload-free bundle: the seed itself must equal the proven hash | `E*` | Asserted with a wrong hash by `BundleLib_Validation.test_runningHashMismatch_reverts`, `BundleLogic_ErrorHandling.test_runningHashMismatch_reverts` and `BundleLib_PartialBundle.test_partialBundle_globalSentRunningHash_causesHashMismatch`. A forged payload is asserted in B13, chained with the message-position guard |
| B4 | Bundle replay / rewind: resubmit a processed bundle, or claim more new messages than payloads carried | Double-dispatch of inbound messages | `BundleLib._validateAndPrepare` Step 4 computes `newMessageCount = nextMessageId - (receivedMessageId + 1)` and raises `ClprReplayDetected` when it is negative or exceeds `messagePayloads.length`, so a payload-free bundle must claim exactly `receivedMessageId + 1`. A count of exactly zero is left to `_checkBundleProgress`, which runs earlier. `messageStartIndex` is the tolerated leading-duplicate window that makes prefix bundles legal | `E` | **Asserted**: `BundleAttacks.test_B4_rewoundBundle_cannotRedeliverAcceptedMessages` for a count behind `receivedMessageId`, the one branch no component suite reaches, and `BundleLib_PartialBundle.test_partialBundle_globalNextMessageId_causesReplayDetected` for a count beyond the payloads. Chained in B13. **Note**: neutering the guard makes the code fall through to `Panic(0x11)`, so these rows must assert a selector, never a bare `vm.expectRevert()` |
| B5 | Message-ID sequencing: gaps, reordering, or replies out of order | Application-visible reordering; silent message loss | Ids are assigned in lockstep in `_dispatchMessages`, so gaps are structurally impossible. Reply ordering is pre-scanned in `_checkResponseOrdering`; a violation on a CLOSING channel reverts `ClprBundleVerificationFailed`, on ACTIVE it moves the channel to PAUSED and returns without dispatching, and on PAUSED it is a no-op. Auto-resume back to ACTIVE is a later step | `E*` | Asserted by `BundleLib_ResponseOrdering.test_responseOrderingViolation_pausesChannel` and `…test_checkResponseOrdering_replyForFutureMessage_causesViolation`. **Note:** `ClprInvalidMessageId` is *not* the bundle-path error; it is redaction-only. A pause is persisted to `_channels`, and the peer's verifier proves that status word directly (`ClprEvmBundleVerifier._channelMetadataSlots`) |
| B6 | Ack forgery: ack a message never sent, or move the ack backwards | Frees queue slots and quota that were never consumed; corrupts in-flight accounting | The ack **is** the peer's proven `QueueMetadata.receivedMessageId`. `_validateAndPrepare` Step 6 rejects a backwards move and an ack at or beyond `channel.nextMessageId` → `ClprAckVerificationFailed`. The value itself is state-proven at the verifier (V7), so it cannot be fabricated | `E*` | Asserted by `BundleLib_Validation.test_ackVerification_ackingUnsentMessages_reverts` and `…test_submitBundle_ackGoesBackward_reverts`. Chained with quota accounting in B14 |
| B7 | Oversized bundle: exhaust gas or memory via blob, message count, or payload size | Block-level DoS; unprocessable channel | `proofBytes.length > maxSyncBytes` → `BundleTooLarge()`, checked in Step 2 **before** the verifier call. `messagePayloads.length > maxMessagesPerBundle` → `TooManyMessages()` and per-payload `> maxMessagePayloadBytes` → `ClprPayloadTooLarge`, both **after** the verifier call. Peer endpoints are truncated rather than rejected via `ClprTypes.truncateEndpoints`. Defaults come from `ClprConfig.defaultThrottles` | `E*` | Asserted twice already, at both levels: `BundleLib_Validation.t.sol` and `BundleLogic_RateLimiting.t.sol`, including the at-limit boundary. **Note** `TooManyMessages` is checked *after* `_checkBundleProgress`, so a bundle that is both oversized and no-progress reports `NoProgress` — advance `nextMessageId` or you assert the wrong error |
| B8 | Malformed protobuf: unknown wire field, bad oneof variant, empty payload, wrong wire type | Decoder confusion; divergent interpretation across implementations | Three errors abort the whole bundle. `ClprUnknownWireField(fieldNumber)` from `ClprProtobuf` is caught and re-thrown byte-for-byte on the DATA and CONTROL paths (`BundleLib._revertWith`), and propagates directly on the REPLY path. `ClprBundleVerificationFailedOneOfVariant` from `decodeControlMessage`. `ClprBundleVerificationFailedEmptyPayload` from `getMessageType`, which is called *outside* the try/catch. REPLY payloads are decoded with no try/catch at all — `_checkResponseOrdering` and `_dispatchMessages` call the decoder directly — so any REPLY decode failure aborts the whole bundle. On the CONTROL path the decoder's own `ClprProtobufHelpers.TruncatedInput` and `VarintOverflow` abort it too. The wire type of a *known* field is not checked: `decodeDataMessage`, `decodeReplyMessage` and `decodeControlMessage` read each field under its expected type, and `ClprEvmBundleVerifier._decodeBundleContent` skips field-2 entries that are not length-delimited. Every *other* DATA decode failure degrades instead to `BundleParseFailed` plus an `APPLICATION_ERROR` reply | `E*` + `N` | Asserted by `BundleLib_ParseFailure.test_parseFailure_data_unknownField_revertsEntireBundle`, `…test_emptyPayload_singleMessage_revertsEntireBundle`, `…test_unknownControlOneOfVariant_revertsEntireBundle` and `…test_parseFailure_control_revertsEntireBundle`. The unchecked wire type of a known field is `N`. Still open for the Vitest layer: the real RPC error surface |
| B12 | Status-transition forgery: drive the channel to CLOSING or CLOSED from the peer side | The peer's proven status (`QueueMetadata.state`) gates the drain sequence and, at CLOSED, the channel's end of life | Peer-driven transitions go through one function, `BundleLib._stepStatus`, shared with `_checkBundleProgress` and `_applyStateMachine` so progress and application cannot disagree. Transitions are monotonic, at most three hops, one event per hop, and gated on `_allDataAcked` / `_allOutboundAcked`. The peer's status is state-proven at the verifier (V7) | `E` | **Asserted end-to-end** by `BundleAttacks.test_B12_closingChannel_cannotBeRevivedByPeerClaims`. `BundleLib_StateMachine.t.sol` covers the same rule exhaustively but against `BundleLibHarness`, i.e. the internal `_stepStatus` rather than the `submitBundle` path |
| B13 | **Chained skip attempt**: try to skip an undelivered message by over-claiming `nextMessageId`, then by forging that message's payload | A skipped message is silent data loss across the ledger boundary. Each guard is tested alone; what is untested is whether a failed attempt leaves the channel able to accept the honest bundle afterwards | Message position (`ClprReplayDetected`) and the hash chain (`ClprRunningHashMismatch`) close the two routes; both revert before any state is written, so `receivedMessageId` and `receivedRunningHash` are unchanged and the honest continuation still applies | `E` | **Asserted**: `BundleAttacks.test_B13_messageCannotBeSkipped_andFailedAttemptsLeaveNoState` — two failed attempts, then proof the honest bundle delivers the message exactly once |
| B14 | **Ack laundering**: free per-connector queue quota with an unbacked ack | Quota is released by the peer's ack. Laundering it would let one connector exceed its share of the shared queue indefinitely, and would drop messages still awaiting a reply | `ClprAckVerificationFailed` bounds the ack by `nextMessageId`, and the value itself is state-proven at the verifier (V7). Quota accounting is only touched on the apply path, which a rejected bundle never reaches | `E` | **Asserted**: `BundleAttacks.test_B14_forgedAck_cannotLaunderConnectorQuota` — quota still exhausted after the attack, and a legitimate ack frees exactly one slot. Documents a non-obvious rule: an ack must arrive *with* the reply for the message it acks, or it is itself an ordering violation and pauses the channel |
| B15 | **Forwarded-gas starvation (63/64 rule)**: submit a bundle with just enough gas that a callback receives less than its configured gas | The EVM forwards at most 63/64 of the remaining gas to a call (EIP-150). A submitter who sizes the transaction so the outer call succeeds while the callback runs short can make an inbound DATA message fail, or a notification not run, and the bundle still applies | **None.** `BundleLib._processDataMessageDecoded` calls `onClprMessage{gas: gasLimit}` without checking that `gasleft()` covers `gasLimit`. An out-of-gas application lands in the `catch`: the message is recorded as `APPLICATION_ERROR` with an empty reason, the connector is charged for the gas used, and the failure is final. The same applies to `onClprResponse{gas: maxGasPerMessage}` in `_processReplyMessageDecoded`, where the source application misses its response, and to `onInboundMessage{gas: connectorInboundGasStipend}` in `_notifyConnectorInbound` | `N` | File issue. Fix direction: revert the bundle when `gasleft()` is below `gasLimit · 64 / 63` plus the call overhead, so the submitter must resend with enough gas |
| B16 | **Submitter-controlled gas price**: set `tx.gasprice` to steer a connector's gas cap, its charge, and the underfunded slash | `submitBundle` is permissionless, and the charge and slash proceeds go to the submitter, so the price it picks decides what a connector pays and whether it is slashed | **None.** `BundleLib._processDataMessageDecoded` uses `tx.gasprice` three ways: `affordableGas = balance · 100 / ((100 + endpointMarginPercent) · tx.gasprice)` caps the application's gas; the charge `gasUsed · tx.gasprice` plus the margin is paid to `ctx.bundleSubmitter`; and `affordableGas == 0` slashes the connector and replies `CONNECTOR_UNDERFUNDED`. That gives three levers. A submitter that recaptures the priority fee (the block producer, a sequencer, or a validator on a zero-base-fee chain) charges at an inflated price and drains the connector's balance. A higher price shrinks the application's gas until it runs out. A price above `balance · 100 / (100 + endpointMarginPercent)` per gas slashes a funded connector and pays the proceeds to the submitter, who must hold `txGas · tx.gasprice` up front but recaptures the priority part | `N` | File issue. Fix direction: bound the price used for accounting (for example `block.basefee` plus a configured tip cap), and do not slash when the shortfall comes from the price |
| B17 | **Unbounded reply**: have the destination application return, or revert with, arbitrarily large data | The return value or revert reason becomes the REPLY payload queued back to the peer. An oversized reply either cannot be delivered or cannot be stored, and either way the channel stops | **None.** `BundleLib._processDataMessageDecoded` passes the application's return data or revert reason to `_enqueueReplyAndEmit`, and `_enqueueMessage` stores it with no length check; replies skip the wedge guard `MessagingLogic.sendMessage` applies to DATA (M5). Two effects. A reply above the peer's `maxMessagePayloadBytes` (default 1,024 bytes) sits at the head of the outbound queue, and no later message on the channel can be delivered past it. A reply of hundreds of kilobytes, reachable within the default `maxGasPerMessage` of 1,000,000, is copied and stored outside the metered `gasUsed`, so the bundle carrying the DATA message no longer fits in a block and the inbound side stops. Any sender with a connector can target such an application | `N` | File issue. Fix direction: cap the reply at what the peer accepts, replacing an oversized response with an error status, and copy return data with a bound |
